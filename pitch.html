Does the circuit explain the effect? A sham-controlled test across every TMS depression trial

A trial-level network meta-regression that uses each trial's own sham arm as a negative control for circuit specificity.

Draft for discussion · November 2026 · for M. D. Fox, N. Williams, J. Taylor, R. Sar-El, S. Siddiqi
Question Across all sham-controlled TMS trials for depression, does the normative connectivity of each trial's stimulation site to the subgenual cingulate predict improvement in the active arm, and not in the sham arm?

Why now

Recent results from this group's own work disagree. sgACC normative connectivity of the treatment site predicted response in a prospective multisite cohort in routine clinical practice [1]. Proximity to an individualized SGC–DLPFC target and e-field modeling did not predict outcome in treatment-resistant depression [2]. Two 2026 randomized trials compared connectivity-guided with conventional targeting of accelerated TMS [3, 4]; the first has drawn a published exchange about protocol deviations.

Individual cohorts cannot settle this. They have narrow ranges of target location, a single site and a single protocol. Published trials, by contrast, span the 5 cm rule, Beam F3, anatomical neuronavigation and connectivity-guided targets, so between-trial variation in connectivity is large. More than 100 sham-controlled RCTs are available [5]. A systematic review of sgACC connectivity as a predictor exists but does not pool data [6].

The core idea: the sham arm as a negative control

Active arms: improvement scales with how strongly the site is anticorrelated with sgACC.

Sham arms: same intended site, no effective stimulation. A circuit-specific effect predicts no slope here.

Schematic of the predicted pattern, not data. A slope in sham arms as well would point to confounding by trial design, era or expectation rather than circuit engagement.

Sham response in TMS depression trials is large and has grown over time [7, 8]. The aim is therefore not only to correlate connectivity with the active–sham difference, but to show the dissociation directly: an arm × connectivity interaction in a multilevel model of arm-level change.

Design

  • Eligibility. Randomized, sham-controlled trials of prefrontal rTMS or TBS (standard or accelerated) in adults with unipolar or bipolar depression. PRISMA 2020, preregistered on PROSPERO.
  • Site per trial. Use reported MNI or Talairach coordinates where available. Otherwise, reconstruct the site from the targeting method using published group-mean coordinates for each heuristic [9]. Code connectivity-guided targets separately. Run sensitivity analyses on trials with reported coordinates only.
  • Connectivity. Normative-connectome sgACC connectivity of each site (sphere and e-field-weighted versions), plus connectivity to the convergent depression circuit derived from lesions and stimulation sites [10].
  • Outcomes. Arm-level standardized change on HAM-D/MADRS, plus response and remission. Where item-level data exist, dysphoric and anxiosomatic subscores as a secondary analysis.
  • Models. Multilevel meta-regression (metafor::rma.mv): arm-level change ~ arm × connectivity, with trial random effects. Covariates: total pulses, sessions, accelerated vs daily, TRD status, blinding method, coil type, publication year. A contrast-level meta-regression on Hedges' g as a check. Risk of bias with RoB 2; certainty with GRADE.

Pre-specified predictions

  1. Stronger site–sgACC anticorrelation predicts larger improvement in active arms.
  2. The same measure does not predict improvement in sham arms (the arm × connectivity interaction is significant).
  3. The association survives adjustment for protocol dose, accelerated delivery and publication year.
  4. Exploratory: anxiosomatic and dysphoric change track different circuits, in trials that report item-level data.

Feasibility and risks

  • Scale. The most recent dose-response meta-analysis included 108 sham-controlled rTMS trials [5]. Theta-burst and accelerated trials add more.
  • Missing coordinates. Many trials report only a targeting method. Mitigation: heuristic reconstruction, plus a coordinates-only sensitivity analysis and author queries.
  • Ecological confounding. Trial-level associations can reflect era, population or protocol. Mitigation: sham-arm negative control, covariate adjustment and a leave-one-targeting-method-out analysis.
  • Overlap. PubMed and PROSPERO searches (November 2026) found no trial-level, arm-level version of this analysis. Unpublished work in your labs would not show up there.
